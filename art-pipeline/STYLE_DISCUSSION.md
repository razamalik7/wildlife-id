## Claude's close-up read: two separate dimensions

**1. Technique (how it is drawn).** Five techniques, from close-up crops:

- **Graphic / vector**: polar bear (heavy even navy outline, cel shading with
  blue shadows), grizzly (smooth clip-art fills).
- **Clean stylized**: clean confident outline, color laid in as broad painted
  fields, texture only where it defines the animal (mane, tail tip, pattern).
  Marten, elk, box turtle, arctic fox, caribou, peregrine falcon, ocelot,
  black bear, harbor seal, eastern newt.
- **Painterly brush**: visible dabs and strokes building the fur/feathers,
  rim light. Gray wolf, rock pigeon, wolverine.
- **Pen-and-ink with color wash**: fine hatched pen lines everywhere over a
  color wash, the classic field-guide plate. Groundhog, fox squirrel, muskox,
  snowy owl, great blue heron, snowshoe hare.
- **Realistic rendered**: soft, photo-like modelling, fine scales/plates,
  little or no outline. Gila monster, armadillo, nile monitor, desert tortoise.

Notes:
- The user's three "loves" and all four "cartoonish stylized" pieces fall
  in the same technique (clean stylized). So do ocelot, harbor seal, newt.
- Clean stylized is the only technique that already spans every class among
  the user's likes: mammals, a bird (falcon), a reptile (box turtle), an
  amphibian (newt) and a pinniped (harbor seal).
- Within it, outline weight varies (turtle/seal bolder, fox lighter) and
  color intensity varies (elk/turtle/newt rich, fox/caribou muted).
- "Textbook" pieces are mostly pen-and-ink or realistic technique combined
  with a specimen pose and low color (hare, crane, anoles, heron).

**2. Life (how present the animal feels).** Independent of technique:

- **Pose**: caught mid-movement or mid-attention (wolf striding, ocelot
  stalking, caribou walking) vs. specimen display (crane wings fanned
  symmetrically, anoles pinned flat to a stick, hare in neutral profile).
- **Gaze / engagement**: head turned toward the viewer or locked on
  something (marten, elk, ocelot, falcon) vs. blank profile.
- **Drama**: strong value contrast, rim light, bold pattern (ocelot, turtle,
  falcon, muskox).
- **Character / humanization**: harbor seal (round eyes, soft smile line:
  cute), fox squirrel (frontal, symmetric, paws clasped like a mascot: likely
  the "something off").
- **Color**: rich color carries the stylized pieces (gila, newt, elk).

## Round 3: Magic vs Stylish vs the combo

User answers:
- Artistic is its own direction; probably not committing to it.
- Friendliness: the marten's level is right. Think Pokémon: not all friendly,
  but all dynamic and real ("ocelot vs jaguar").
- Life = pose + depth together. A standard-ish pose is fine: marten,
  elephant seal, falcon, nile monitor, fox squirrel, arctic fox, crane
  (somewhat), polar bear, snowy owl, wolverine, caribou, armadillo all have
  good poses without doing too much. Jaguar and rattlesnake not loved despite
  their poses. Gila: very standard pose, but real depth and the tail curl.
- Nearly all the pieces that feel alive were generated from a real reference
  photo.

Findings:
- The combo signature (flat calm fields + rich color) also matches the
  rejected grizzly, so the numbers can't separate the combo from flat
  cartoon. The difference is depth.
- A blurred large-scale shading measure did NOT capture depth: plastic pieces
  (grizzly, polar bear, sea lion) shade as much as the elk. The difference is
  the kind of shading: painted planes and texture following the form, true
  darks, overlaps (gila tail curl) vs smooth airbrushed gradients. Eye
  judgment for now. Rattlesnake did measure the flattest of all tested.
- The combo exemplars (marten, elk, white-tailed deer, axis deer) are all
  mammals: no evidence yet that the combo works for birds or reptiles.
  Magic has strong reptile evidence; the falcon is the best bird evidence.
- Reframe: Stylish -> combo -> Magic is one dial (how much surface material
  is rendered), with the user's loves spanning marten/elk to
  ocelot/turtle/gila. Constants across the dial: painted depth (not
  airbrush), clean thin outline, rich value range, living pose.

## Round 4: art features vs animal features

User placements: snowy owl -> maybe combo; mountain goat and caribou close
to combo; box turtle maybe combo; nile monitor is the ceiling of Magic, a
little too realistic; armadillo close to that.

User concern (valid): earlier sets tracked animal coat - brown animals in
combo, white in stylish-only, intricate patterns (and small animals, more
striking when scaled up in a tile) in Magic.

Matched same-coat sets (style_review/matched_sets.jpg) show art varies a lot
with coat held constant: big plain-brown mammals (elk calm, moose strokes
everywhere, grizzly airbrush, bison heavy texture); white mammals (fox
minimal, goat hair everywhere, polar bear sticker, hare textbook); spotted
cats (ocelot spots wrap the form, jaguar's flatten it); axis deer shows the
combo can handle pattern.

Art-level definition of the dial: WHERE detail is rendered. Combo renders
material (strands, scales) only along edges and at focal areas (face, mane,
tail tip) and leaves big body surfaces as calm painted tone; Magic renders
material across the whole body.

Measured "interior detail / edge detail" ratio (fine strokes only, pattern
boundaries excluded): combo-read pieces 0.43-0.80 (white-tailed deer 0.43,
elk 0.52, axis 0.58, owl 0.65, caribou 0.74, marten 0.79, turtle 0.80);
ocelot 0.85, armadillo 0.92, monitor 1.09, gila 1.27; goat and hare 0.87
(hair everywhere); arctic fox 0.80, bison 0.83, moose 0.61. Points the right
way but overlaps - supporting evidence only.

Coat-confounded measurements (treat with care): saturation, warmth, flat
areas (plain coats), drawn lines / fine texture (patterns), contrast and
shadow depth (white and black coats). More coat-robust: outline thickness,
darkness and coverage; silhouette roughness; detail distribution.

Tile scale: normalization makes every animal fill the tile the same, so
small animals' detail is magnified and long thin animals (gila, snakes,
monitor) shrink. Affects how striking pieces look in review and in the Dex.
Detail density should be specified relative to the tile; true size handled
in post from body-length data (as planned); consider area-based fill for
long animals.

Revised read (pending user):
- Too realistic: nile monitor, armadillo
- Magic: ocelot, gila
- Combo: marten, elk, caribou, axis deer, white-tailed deer, snowy owl,
  box turtle (shell combo, skin Magic)
- Stylish, short on depth: arctic fox, black bear, polar bear (sticker)
- Unclear: mountain goat (hair rendered everywhere, weak depth)

## Round 7: gap-class rankings and "color depth"

- Macaque -> combo. Hurt only by its real-life colors; rendered well.
- Axis deer has some depth. Box turtle: legs lack depth, but the shell's
  colors and pattern give it real depth, which is what makes it stand out.
- **Color depth vs blendy (user):** our shading is color gradients and
  highlights (ocelot, box turtle shell, marten), not blended paint (the
  swan's head). Working wording: shading built from distinct color steps and
  highlights, where you can see one tone end and the next begin - vs soft
  smudged transitions mixing toward mud. A shadow-color-retention measure did
  not separate the groups (pattern and white coats confound it); eye judgment.

Gap-class rankings (user):
1. Amphibians: none liked; spotted salamander if forced. Lizard-like ones
   too textbooky; frog and toad too flat.
2. Small lizards: none loved; brown and green anole top two, but want more
   contrast between stick and lizard. Tegu too flat despite pose; iguana too
   arty; tokay far too 3D-rendered; chameleon a bit too detailed.
3. Snakes: none.
4. Small birds: none quite right, a bit arty. Pigeon maybe fits; parakeet
   maybe good; starling good but too detailed and dark (textbooky); blue jay
   a bit flat; cardinal very textbooky; sparrow a bit too arty.
5. Pinnipeds: harbor seal good (a bit cartoony; likes the spots and skin
   texture the others lack) > elephant seal > walrus?; sea lion worst - should
   have some fur texture and has none.
6. Large birds: flamingo far too flat; heron too artsy and flat; pelican and
   crane closest but a bit dead; turkey too 3D-lit; goose weirdly shaded
   (worst); swan body OK but head blended-painty.

Read: the target is narrow, with misses on every side - too flat (frog,
toad, tegu, flamingo, blue jay), too 3D (tokay, turkey), too detailed
(chameleon, starling), too arty (iguana, sparrow, heron), textbooky (most
lizards, cardinal), blendy/odd shading (swan head, goose), dead (pelican,
crane). Usable anchors exist only partially: harbor seal (pinnipeds), pigeon
and maybe parakeet (small birds), pelican/crane closest (large birds). None
for amphibians, snakes or lizards. These misses are useful as per-class
negative examples.

Vignette rule from the anoles: the prop must contrast with (be quieter
than) the animal.

Current combo set: marten, elk, caribou, axis deer, white-tailed deer, snowy
owl, box turtle, desert tortoise, armadillo, mountain goat (a bit off),
rhesus macaque.
